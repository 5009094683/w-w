## What technical consulting is

Technical consulting is advice from engineers who are not part of your team: a second opinion on a plan, a review of something already built, or help with a decision that is expensive to get wrong. The consultant does not take the project over. Your team keeps ownership and makes the final call.

It is most useful at a few moments: before a large investment, when a system has stopped scaling, when costs are rising without a clear reason, or when a team is adopting a technology it has not used before.

## How we work with your team

Our goal is to share our expertise and domain knowledge with your team, tackling tough issues right by your side. We collaborate with your team on many levels, such as:

- Providing technical guidance and architectural reviews
- Advising on best practices
- Solving design decisions, and more

Let us help take your application to the next level: planning the next big steps, reviewing architecture and brainstorming with the team so that you reach your most ambitious goals.

## Topics we advise on

- **AI:** where AI fits in your product or operations, and how to introduce it.
- **Technical audit:** an outside review of an existing system.
- **Tech architecture development:** shaping how a system is structured before or while it is built.
- **Cloud cost saving:** finding where cloud spending can come down.
- **Brand development and business development:** advice beyond the codebase, for technology companies.

## Who it is for

- Founders and product leaders who want an experienced engineer's view before committing to a direction.
- Engineering teams that want an independent review of their architecture or practices.
- Companies whose cloud bill or technical debt has grown faster than the product.

## What you get

The outcome of a consulting engagement is a clearer decision, not a pile of code. Depending on the question, that can be a written review, a recommended architecture, a list of changes in priority order, or working sessions with your team. The specific deliverables and timeline are set out in a Statement of Work before the work starts, as described in our [Terms & Conditions](/terms-conditions).
